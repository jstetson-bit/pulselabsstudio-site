PLS Authenticator: Privacy Policy

Pulse Labs Studio · Effective November 21, 2026

Short version: PLS Authenticator has no internet permission, so Android does not let it go online. It collects nothing and sends nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no cloud copy, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. Pulse Labs never receives, stores, sells or shares your codes, your passwords or anything about how you use the app.

What PLS Authenticator does

It keeps your two-factor sign-in codes, and (after an optional one-time unlock) your passwords, in one encrypted vault file on your device. Only your master password or your recovery key opens it.

What stays on your device

  • Your vault: accounts, code secrets, saved passwords, usernames, websites and notes. It is encrypted (AES-256-GCM, with a key protected by your master password and by your recovery key) and stored in the app's private storage.
  • Your settings: look, text size, auto-lock time and similar choices.
  • The crash log: if the app ever crashes, technical details of the crash are kept on the device so you can include them in a problem report. It never contains your vault's contents.
  • The app is excluded from Android and Google backups on purpose, so your vault is never copied to a cloud by the system. Backups happen only when you export one yourself: an encrypted file that goes only where you save it.

Permissions, and what each one is for

  • Camera: scans the QR code a website shows when you add a two-factor account. Pictures are read on the device for the code and are never saved or sent. You can type the setup key instead.
  • Fingerprint or face unlock (biometric): optional, to open the vault without typing your master password. Android performs the check; the app never sees or stores your fingerprint or face.
  • Google Play billing: sells the optional one-time password-vault unlock. The payment is handled by the Google Play Store app, under Google's own privacy policy. PLS Authenticator only checks Google's signed receipt on your device. Pulse Labs does not receive your card or payment details.

Filling in logins (autofill)

If you choose PLS Authenticator as your device's autofill service, Android tells it when another app or a website shows a sign-in form, and which website or app that form belongs to. PLS Authenticator uses that, on the device, only to offer the matching saved login after you unlock it. It does not record what you type or what is on your screen, and it sends nothing anywhere. Apps you link to a login are remembered inside your encrypted vault.

Opening websites and Google Play

When you tap "Open" on a saved website, or "Check for updates", PLS Authenticator asks your browser or the Google Play Store app to open that page. Those apps make the connection, not PLS Authenticator, under their own privacy policies.

Problem reports

"Report a Problem" prepares an email in your own email app, to support@pulselabsstudio.com, with what you wrote, the app version, your device model and Android version, your look and text-size settings, and the crash log. You see it before anything is sent, and nothing is sent unless you send it. Your vault's contents are never included. We use a report only to answer you and fix the problem.

Children

PLS Authenticator collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Removing your data

Uninstalling the app removes the vault, your settings and the crash log from the device. Backup files you exported stay where you saved them until you delete them. Pulse Labs holds no copy of anything, so there is nothing for us to delete.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new effective date.
